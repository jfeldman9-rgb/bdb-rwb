## Findings (prioritized)

### F1 (P2). Nightmare S3 equalization landed at the slow end — 30/120 Hz moved to 60 Hz, not the reverse
My own runs on both commits, same harness (`tools/bot-sim.mjs nightmare`, fresh stage starts):

| Rate | Pass 4 (`3f76903`) | Pass 5 (`a8a103c`) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| S3 @ 30 Hz | 283.9 s / 7 d | 326.6 s / 10 d | **+15% time, +3 deaths** |
| S3 @ 60 Hz | 320.0 s / 11 d | 326.1 s / 10 d | +1.9% time, −1 death |
| S3 @ 120 Hz | 271.4 s / 7 d | 328.4 s / 11 d | **+21% time, +4 deaths** |

The outlier is gone — all three rates now agree to within 2.3 s — but they agreed at ~326–328 s, i.e. Nightmare S3 as a whole became slower and more lethal at the two rates that were previously healthy. This cannot be the snowball thresholds themselves (65 → 58/50 px *lowers* the dodge bar — `src/campaign.js:27-31,394`), so the drag is coming from the P5-5 separation/telegraph geometry and the phase-3 finisher/Dad path in the Wingman fight. Nothing locked was touched (no Choirmaster/Wingman numbers; the 140–240 s band is Adventure-scoped), all stages still clear at all rates, and the fix-list did call for matching 60 Hz — but "finishable is not the same as fun" (Muse, pass 4) now applies more strongly, and the direction should be a conscious call (ideally Jason's, alongside the deferred Choirmaster numbers), not an accident of the snowball constant. Suggest: one investigation of what in S3 got slower at 30/120 Hz, and pin the Nightmare S3 multi-rate table in a test the way `tests/nightmare-clear.test.mjs` pins clears, so the next pass sees the drift before review does.
